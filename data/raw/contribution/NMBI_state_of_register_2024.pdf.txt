Dr Ray Healy Director of Registration 4 5 NMBI State of the Register 2024 Overview of the NMBI Register As of 1 June 2024, there are 89,496 nurses and midwives registered with NMBI. This represents a 5% increase on the previous year and is the largest number of registrants ever held by NMBI. Of these, 84,213 state that they are currently practising and 76,054 state they are patient- facing in their role. In comparison to figures from the State of the Register 2023, the number of practising nurses and midwives has increased by 6% and those who are patient-facing has increased by 7%. Over the three-year period covered in this report, the number of registered, practising and patient-facing is increasing. On a divisional level, most divisions saw an increase in the number of registered, practising and patient-facing nurses and midwives. There was an increase in the number of registered General Nurses (up 6%); Children’s Nurses (up 1%) and Psychiatric Nurses (up 1%). While the overall number of registered Intellectual Disability Nurses has declined slightly (down 0.2%), the number of practising and patient-facing has increased. Similarly, there was a decrease in the number registered in the Midwives division (down 3%), but the number of those practising and patient-facing increased. Overall, there has been a 14% increase in the number of new nurses and midwives joining the Register. New registrants include nurses and midwives qualified in Ireland, the EU and non-EU countries. In the period of this report, 1,569 new registrants qualified in Ireland (down 1%), 5,426 qualified outside the EU (up 19%) and 125 qualified in EU countries (down 5%). It is important to note that since 2022, UK registrants are no longer included in the EU figures. Nurses and midwives who are educated outside of Ireland play an essential role in the Irish health system. The top countries of education of new overseas registrants were India (3,717), Philippines (828), United Kingdom (281) and Ghana (191). The report shows a steady increase in the number of registrations in the post-primary divisions of the Register. Since 2023, there was a 20% increase in the number of Advanced Nurse Practitioners and a 23% increase in Advanced Midwife Practitioners. Nurse Prescribers increased by 16%, and Midwife Prescribers increased by 27%. The number of Public Health Nurses also increased by 1%. From 1 June 2023 to 31 May 2024, NMBI issued 5,073 Decision Letters to overseas applicants. In the first stage, Recognition of Qualifications, an NMBI Education Assessor will assess evidence of the applicant’s education, qualifications and credentials against the NMBI standards and requirements to practise in Ireland. Once the application has been assessed, NMBI will issue the applicant with a Decision Letter. The Decision Letter will inform the applicant that either their education, qualifications and credentials meet the NMBI standards and requirements to register, or that there is a requirement to complete a compensation measure. A compensation measure is either a period of paid adaptation undertaken at an approved Irish healthcare facility and subject to an assessment, or an aptitude test that is successfully completed. The compensation measure allows an applicant to demonstrate their competency to meet the NMBI standards and requirements. Once the applicant has completed the compensation measure successfully, the recognition process is complete and they can apply for registration.
